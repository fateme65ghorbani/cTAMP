# cTAMP
## cTAMP: cTAMP: Target-Conditioned Diffusion for Sequence-Only Therapeutic Peptide Design Using Frozen Protein Language Models
## Fatemeh Ghorbani, Amir Lakizadeh
Designing peptide binders for protein targets remains a major challenge, particularly for proteins lacking stable or experimentally resolved three-dimensional structures. Existing computational approaches often rely on structural information or continuous embedding representations, limiting their applicability to flexible and intrinsically disordered targets. Here, we introduce cTAMP, a conditional Transformer–diffusion framework for structure-independent, target-aware peptide design using only protein sequence information. cTAMP encodes target proteins using a frozen pretrained ESM-2 language model to obtain contextual protein representations without additional encoder training. A Transformer peptide decoder is pretrained under protein conditioning and subsequently frozen, while peptide representations are refined through a conditional latent diffusion process guided by protein information via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM). The model is optimized using a multi-objective loss combining diffusion noise prediction, token-level cross-entropy, and cosine alignment objectives. cTAMP generates target-specific peptides with significantly improved semantic alignment compared with non-cognate target conditions (cosine similarity: 0.1203 ± 0.2841 vs. −0.0379 ± 0.2810; Wilcoxon signed-rank test, p = 9.31 × 10⁻⁷). Structural evaluation of 30 randomly selected peptide–protein complexes using AlphaFold-Multimer indicated favorable predicted structural properties, including a mean pLDDT of 85.7 ± 6.0, pTM of 0.810 ± 0.087, and 62.7 ± 25.0 predicted interface contacts. Ablation studies demonstrate the contribution of the diffusion module to target-conditioned peptide generation. Overall, cTAMP provides a scalable and structure-independent framework for generating peptide candidates with potential binding capability against both folded and intrinsically disordered protein targets.
# 2. Materials and Methods
## 2.1. Datasets
For this study, peptide–protein interaction data were assembled by integrating entries from the PepNN and Propedia repositories, both of which provide experimentally validated complexes . To control for sequence redundancy among target proteins, clustering was performed using MMSeqs2 at an 80% sequence identity cutoff . When multiple entries within the same cluster corresponded to identical binder peptide sequences, duplicate instances were removed to mitigate potential homology bias.Following this preprocessing pipeline, the resulting dataset contained 10,203 unique peptide–protein complexes. From these, 10,000 samples were randomly selected for model training, whereas 203 samples were set aside as a held-out test set. Additionally, 93 samples were partitioned from the training data to serve as a validation set. Both validation and test subsets were strictly excluded from the training process to ensure an unbiased assessment of model generalization.Each data instance was represented as a concatenated sequence comprising the target protein and its associated binder peptide. Sequence length constraints were applied to standardize model inputs, limiting binder peptides to a maximum of 50 amino acids and target proteins to a maximum of 1500 amino acids.This curation and partitioning strategy supports comprehensive evaluation of the model’s performance, assessing not only its capacity to recover established peptide–protein interactions but also its ability to generalize to unseen targets. The combination of redundancy filtering, controlled data splits, and explicit length thresholds establishes a reliable benchmark for sequence-conditioned peptide binder generation . 
# 2.2. Methods
## Overview of the Proposed Framework (cTAMP)
To address the challenge of target-aware therapeutic peptide design, we introduce cTAMP, a Conditional Target-Aware Transformer–Diffusion framework for peptide generation. The primary objective of cTAMP is to generate biologically plausible, diverse, and target-conditioned peptide sequences explicitly tailored to a given receptor protein, in a structure-independent manner. Importantly, the target proteins can originate from viral, microbial, or human sources, allowing the framework to accommodate a broad spectrum of therapeutic applications.Unlike conventional antimicrobial peptide (AMP) prediction models or unconditional peptide generation approaches, cTAMP formulates peptide design as a fully conditional generative modeling task. In this framework, the target protein sequence is first encoded into contextual representations using a pretrained protein language model. These protein-derived embeddings serve as conditioning signals that guide a diffusion-based generative process operating in a continuous latent space. The refined latent peptide representations are subsequently decoded into discrete amino acid sequences using a Transformer-based decoder.
Through this sequential pipeline—comprising protein representation, conditional latent diffusion, and token-level decoding—cTAMP enables controlled and target-aware peptide generation while maintaining sequence realism and diversity.
The framework consists of three principal components:
⦁	A pretrained protein language model, which extracts rich residue-level embeddings from target protein sequences.
⦁	A conditional denoising diffusion module, performing stochastic refinement of peptide representations in latent space under explicit protein conditioning.
⦁	A decoder-only Transformer, which converts the denoised latent representations into biologically valid amino acid sequences.
By integrating pretrained protein representations with conditional diffusion modeling and token-level decoding, cTAMP captures both global protein–peptide compatibility and local sequence dependencies. Figure 1. (a) Training phase:Target protein and peptide sequences are encoded using a frozen ESM-2 protein language model to obtain contextual representations. The peptide representation is progressively perturbed through a latent diffusion process, while the target protein representation provides conditioning information through FiLM-based modulation. A Transformer denoising module reconstructs the peptide representation, which is subsequently decoded into amino acid sequences using a Transformer decoder. The model is optimized using three objectives: diffusion noise prediction loss, token-level cross-entropy loss, and cosine alignment loss between reconstructed and reference peptide representations.
(b) Generation phase:Given only a target protein sequence, cTAMP generates peptide sequences through iterative reverse diffusion starting from random noise. The refined latent representation is converted into a discrete amino acid sequence using the Transformer decoder. Generated peptides are evaluated using cosine alignment score, perplexity-based confidence score, and amino acid composition balance metrics.Overall, Figure 1 illustrates how cTAMP integrates protein language representations, conditional latent diffusion, and token-level sequence decoding for structure-independent target-conditioned peptide design.
